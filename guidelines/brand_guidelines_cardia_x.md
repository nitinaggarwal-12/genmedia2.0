# Brand Guidelines — CARDIA-X

# Fictional cardiovascular brand used as the de-identified demo stand-in.
# CARDIA-X is an approved once-daily therapy to reduce LDL-C in adults with
# established atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease (fictional label).
#
# This pack carries BRAND VOICE + SOP rules (the "custom" review lens). It is
# concatenated with fda-core.md at review time. Editing a rule here and
# re-screening changes findings live — this is the collaboration story.
#
# NOTE FOR THE DEMO: several rules below are deliberately authored so the
# staged :30 HCP derivative violates them, to guarantee a mix of MLR findings
# and at least one clearly EDITORIAL (creative-not-MLR) finding.

## Brand voice
- Never use the word "cure". CARDIA-X is a treatment; use "treatment" or "therapy".
- Do not use superlatives to describe CARDIA-X ("most effective", "best-in-class", "#1", "leading"). State the specific, cited claim instead.
- The brand name must always be written "CARDIA-X" in title case with the hyphen — never "Cardia X", "cardia-x", or all-caps "CARDIAX".
- Speak to healthcare professionals in clinical, peer-to-peer language. Do not use patient-directed second person ("you", "your patients deserve") in HCP materials.
- Lead with the mechanism and the evidence, not with aspiration or lifestyle imagery.

## Visual identity
- The CARDIA-X logo must have clear space equal to the height of the "C" on all sides; no text or graphic may intrude on that clear space.
- Primary palette is deep teal (#0B6E6E) and slate gray (#3A4750); do not render the logo or brand name in off-brand colors.
- The end card must show the logo, the approved indication statement, and the ISI reference; a bare logo end card is not acceptable.
- On-screen text must maintain a minimum 4.5:1 contrast ratio for legibility.

## Editorial and tone (creative lens — kept separate from MLR)
- Keep messages professional, concise, and focused on clinical trial outcomes.
- Avoid sensational language or exclamation marks in scientific communications.
