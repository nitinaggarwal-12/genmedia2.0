# MLR Rules — FDA Core (Pharma Generic)

# Pharma-generic pre-screen ruleset for promotional materials, grounded in FDA
# promotional-labeling principles (21 CFR 202.1; OPDP guidance). This pack is
# CLIENT-AGNOSTIC — brand voice and SOP rules live in the per-brand pack.
#
# Format: free text. Each directive is one rule. `### Section` headers group
# rules by topic; the section name is carried into a finding's principle. Lines
# beginning with `#` are comments and are ignored by the reviewer.
#
# This is a demo accelerator, NOT legal advice or an authoritative MLR ruleset.
# It never replaces formal MLR/MAC review.

## Fair balance and risk presentation
- Any presentation of efficacy or benefit must be accompanied by a comparable presentation of risk information; benefit and risk must have similar prominence, readability, and dwell time.
- Important Safety Information (ISI) must be present on any surface that makes an efficacy claim; it may not be deferred to a separate surface the viewer is not required to see.
- Risk information must not be relegated to fine print, fleeting super, or audio-only when the benefit is presented visually.
- Contraindications and boxed warnings, where applicable, must appear before or alongside the first efficacy claim, never only after it.
- The most common adverse reactions (incidence >= 5% in the pivotal trial) must be named on the same surface as the headline efficacy claim.

## Claim substantiation
- Every efficacy claim must be substantiated by adequate and well-controlled evidence, with the citation shown at the point of the claim (not in an unlinked footnote).
- Relative-risk-reduction claims must be accompanied by the corresponding absolute-risk reduction; do not present relative risk alone.
- Comparative claims (superiority, non-inferiority, "more than", "faster than") require a head-to-head adequate and well-controlled trial; do not imply comparison from separate studies.
- Subgroup or post-hoc claims must be labeled as such and must not be presented as primary-endpoint findings.
- Data from a single study must not be described in terms ("proven", "established") that imply clinical finality beyond the actual data.
